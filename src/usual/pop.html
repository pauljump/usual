What à la carte means here

It is a real boundary, not a marketing word. An à la carte piece has to stand on its own:

  • It installs by itself. You never have to install Usual first.
  • It stores nothing. No account, no database, no mined history, no telemetry.
  • It is legible. The whole thing is instructions you can read before you run them.
  • It uninstalls by deleting the lines it added to your CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md.

These are instructions, not software. An à la carte piece changes how your coding agent writes to you. It does not run a service, intercept commands, or replace your client's permission controls.

If you want the whole thing

Usual proper is the larger idea: your past coding decisions, mined locally, carried into future builds so your agent stops asking you questions you already answered. It shares the same boundaries — local, readable, no hosted inference.
