## Legal discussion: qualified, not a finding of criminality

The user asked whether the observed behavior is illegal. The answer was:
requesting public paths is not automatically criminal; knowingly using another
party's credentials to obtain unauthorized access can violate U.S. law, and the
CFAA also covers attempts. Our logs establish submission of planted credentials
to our decoy, not successful intrusion into real accounts, real AI charges,
identity, or criminal intent. Deliberately supplied credentials/endpoints make
permission and intent especially fact-dependent. Jurisdiction is unresolved.
Use “observed reuse of planted credentials”; do not call identifiable parties
criminals on this evidence. This was general information, not a legal opinion.
